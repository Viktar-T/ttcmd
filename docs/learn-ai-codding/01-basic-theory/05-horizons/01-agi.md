## What it is

**Artificial General Intelligence** (AGI, strong AI, general AI, universal AI) is a hypothetical class of models and/or AI assistants able to reason, learn, and act with the same universal flexibility as a human.

Unlike today’s narrow AI systems, which excel only at specific tasks (such as translation, summarization, or code generation), AGI would be able to solve problems in any domain, adapt to new situations, and improve over time without being directly reprogrammed.

AGI implies:

- **Generalization:** transferring knowledge across domains
- **Autonomy:** acting and planning without human intervention
- **Self-improvement:** iteratively improving its own architecture or skills
- **Self-learning / continual learning:** learning continuously from new data or tasks without retraining from scratch
- **Emergent cognition:** understanding, abstraction, and common sense in reasoning

AGI does not exist yet, but many leading labs — including OpenAI, DeepMind, Anthropic, xAI, and others — have openly said that building AGI is part of their long-term plans. What AGI would look like, how it might arise, and whether it would be safe remain open research questions.

For a developer, AGI is not just a technical endpoint. It is a conceptual horizon that shapes how we think about autonomy, alignment, and responsibility in today’s AI systems.

## How to measure progress toward AGI?

Because AGI does not exist yet, researchers use **proxy benchmarks** to estimate how close we are. They are not perfect, but they give a sense of how universal and how capable of reasoning today’s models are.

The best-known include:

- **ARC Challenge** (AI Reasoning Challenge) — focuses on common sense and solving tasks with minimal context.
- **HLE** (Humanity’s Last Exam) — a benchmark for long-context reasoning and hard deduction.
- **MMLU** (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) — covers 50+ subjects from medicine to law.
- **GPQA** — graduate-level questions that cannot be googled and need real domain knowledge.
- **BigBench, HELM, AGIEval, TPTU** — test suites for generalization and robustness.

Even perfect scores on these tests do not guarantee AGI — but consistently high scores hint at systems with more general cognitive abilities.

## Remote AGI

**Remote AGI** is a concept proposed by Leopold Aschenbrenner in [“Situational Awareness”](https://situational-awareness.ai/). It describes a realistic, near-term path to powerful AI — one that can do any intellectual task you can do remotely on a computer.

Unlike full AGI, which would work in the physical world (robots, factories, and so on), Remote AGI only needs to act in the **digital** world. That means writing code, managing documents, analyzing data, research, and running companies — all through a computer interface.

Remote AGI would:

- live in the cloud and reach APIs, websites, documents, codebases, and software systems
- replace entire teams of workers — write software, do research, run operations
- self-improve and learn new skills, plan projects, and work toward long-term goals

In Aschenbrenner’s view, Remote AGI is much easier to build than full AGI, because most office work already happens on a screen. It is simpler to teach AI to use a computer than to give it a body.

So Remote AGI may be the first AGI that truly changes the world — not by doing everything humans do, but by automating any work that happens online.
