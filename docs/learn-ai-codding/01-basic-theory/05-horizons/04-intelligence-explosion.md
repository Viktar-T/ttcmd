## The core idea

The idea of an **intelligence explosion** was proposed by the British mathematician and cryptographer **Irving John Good** in 1965.

*Portrait on the platform: Irving John Good, 1979 — paste it here if you want it in the local notes.*

He put it like this (original English; the course showed a Russian translation):

> Let an ultraintelligent machine be defined as a machine that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of any man however clever. Since the design of machines is one of these intellectual activities, an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines; there would then unquestionably be an “intelligence explosion,” and the intelligence of man would be left far behind. Thus the first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make.

That idea is the intelligence explosion: a **feedback loop** where a machine improves itself → gets smarter → improves itself faster → gets even smarter — which leads to rapid, uncontrolled growth of intelligence.

## How it relates to other concepts

**[AGI](01-agi.md)**  
AGI is an AI system that can do any intellectual task a human can.

It is the first step toward an intelligence explosion.

Once AGI can self-improve, the loop starts.

**[ASI](02-asi.md)**  
ASI is a level of intelligence far beyond the best human minds.

It is the **end result** of an intelligence explosion — a system that outdoes humans in every domain (science, strategy, creativity, and so on).

**[Technological singularity](03-technological-singularity.md)**  
A hypothetical point where technological growth becomes uncontrolled and irreversible, changing civilization in unpredictable ways.

The intelligence explosion is one of the main **paths** to the singularity.

Once ASI appears, it could create innovations so fast that people can no longer keep up — that is the singularity.

## Why it matters

Good’s idea is one of the founding theories behind modern concerns about [AI safety](12-ai-safety.md) and [alignment](13-ai-alignment.md):

- How do we control AI that is smarter than we are?
- How do we align its goals with human values?
- How do we prepare for a future we may not be able to understand or influence?
