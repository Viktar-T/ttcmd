# Transhumanism and human augmentation

**Source (RU):** Трансгуманизм и человеческая аугментация  
**Path:** Home → Basic Theory → Level “Horizons” → Transhumanism and human augmentation  
**Published:** ~10 months ago

As we get closer to advanced AI — such as [AGI](01-agi.md) and possibly [ASI](02-asi.md) — many thinkers raise another important question: what happens to humans in a world where machines are smarter than we are? That is where the idea of **transhumanism** comes in.

**Transhumanism** is a philosophical and scientific movement that supports using technology to improve the human body and mind. That includes not only treating disease, but expanding memory, intelligence, physical strength, and even lifespan.

Related to this is **human augmentation** — using tools, implants, or software to push human abilities beyond natural limits.

Key possibilities include:

- **Brain-computer interfaces (BCI)** — technologies that connect the brain directly to computers (for example Neuralink)
- **Prosthetics and exoskeletons** — devices that restore or amplify mobility and strength
- **Cognitive enhancers** — drugs or neural tools to improve memory, focus, and learning
- **Mind uploading** — transferring human consciousness into a digital form. Still only theoretical.

In the context of AGI and ASI, some people think augmentation may be the only way for humans to stay relevant — or even survive.

Supporters of transhumanism see this as a path to a **posthuman** future, where people are no longer limited by biology. Critics, however, worry about inequality, ethics, and identity — and whether we should try to merge with machines at all.

The debate is complex, but one thing is clear:

As AI gets smarter, the question of what it means to be human becomes more urgent.
