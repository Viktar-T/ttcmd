## What it is about

As AI becomes more intelligent, we also expect it to become more **aware** — not only of language or [space](07-spatial-intelligence.md), but of the real world at a deeper, more human level. To go beyond narrow tasks and surface answers, AI needs more than data — it needs **understanding**.

That leads to the next big idea: a **General World Model (GWM)**.

A **General World Model (GWM)** — also known as a **Large World Model (LWM)** — is a vision of AI systems that can build a rich, connected understanding of the physical and social world.

Unlike models that only process language or single images, a GWM learns from many types of input at once: images, video, sound, text, and more.

The goal is to teach the model:

- **Causal reasoning** — understanding not only what is happening, but **why**
- **Conceptual abstraction** — seeing patterns and meaning behind the surface
- **Social awareness** — understanding people, interactions, norms, and culture
- **Explainability** — giving answers that reflect deep internal models of reality

Such a model could be a key milestone on the path to true general intelligence — making [AGI](01-agi.md) not only powerful, but **rooted in the real world**.

## Early experiments

Full General World Models do not exist yet, but we already see early examples that simulate small parts of the physical world.

Some AI agents train inside game environments such as **Minecraft** or **MuJoCo**. These agents do not just recognize objects — they learn how the world is put together:

- In **Minecraft**, models can understand how blocks behave, how tools are used, and how the world changes over time. Projects like **MineDojo** combine video, language, and action data to teach agents to act in open-ended ways.
- In **MuJoCo**, AI learns to control physical bodies — balance, walk, jump — by predicting motion and force in simulated environments.

Some experimental models can even generate entire worlds, then let agents move and act inside them. For example, Google DeepMind research has explored using video-generation models to create playable scenes that are both visual and interactive.

## Video generation as world modeling

New models such as **Sora** (OpenAI), **Veo** (Google), and **Pika** (indie) are pushing the limits of video generation. They simulate complex scenes with moving people, objects, shadows, and textures — all with realistic physics and coherence over time.

These models are not full world models yet, but they point the same way: AI that can see, predict, and eventually understand the dynamics of the world — not only in words, but in moving images.
